🌍 The Puzzle ›
When the US Fed raises interest rates, every country feels it — but not equally. India feels 41 bps of shock from a 100 bps hike; Singapore feels 558 bps. Same hike. 13× difference. Why?
👥 Population Weight ›
A country's share of world population determines how much it absorbs vs amplifies US monetary shocks. Large countries have deeper capital markets and systemic protection. Small countries ride the waves. There is a tipping point at ~0.5% of world population — below it, vulnerability becomes self-reinforcing.
🎮 The Strategic Game ›
Central banks react to the Fed and to each other. Modelling this as a formal game reveals a prisoner's dilemma: non-cooperative play drives global rates too high — everyone loses.
⚖️ Nash vs Kant ›
Nash equilibrium (selfish) → 5.11% global rate.
Kant equilibrium (cooperative) → 3.91%.

Welfare gain from cooperation: 27–39% for all countries. Kantian cooperation is mathematically superior, not just ethically appealing.

WELFARE GAIN FROM KANT COOPERATION
MIXED-STRATEGY NASH

When central banks face uncertainty about each other's priorities, they play a mixed strategy. The paper proves this converges to a normal distribution around the pure Nash rate — uncertainty adds noise but preserves the fundamental inefficiency of non-cooperation.

KEY THEOREMS (PLAIN LANGUAGE)
Theorem 1 (Nash): A unique pure-strategy Nash equilibrium exists. Larger countries set higher rates. All rates exceed the Kant optimum.

Theorem 2 (Kant): A Kant equilibrium exists at 3.91%, strictly below the Nash rates. It is the solution to the universal adoption question.

Theorem 3 (Pareto): Kant strictly Pareto-dominates Nash — every single country does better under cooperation.
